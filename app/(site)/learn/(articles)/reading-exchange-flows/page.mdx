# Reading exchange deposits and withdrawals

When a large wallet sends tokens to an exchange, you see a **deposit**. When it takes them off, it's a **withdrawal**. We recognize these using a list of known wallets of Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX and Bybit.

## The common reading

On an exchange you can sell; in your own wallet you can't directly. That's why a large deposit is often read as "maybe someone wants to sell", and a large withdrawal as "someone is moving it off the exchange to hold it". That's a reasonable first question, not a conclusion.

## Why it's often something else

- **Internal moves.** Exchanges shuffle between their own hot and cold wallets. That's why we filter out moves between wallets of the same owner.
- **Collateral.** Tokens on an exchange can serve as collateral for derivatives without being sold.
- **OTC and clients.** Market makers move funds for clients, not for themselves.
- **Stablecoins.** A large USDC deposit can mean someone *wants to buy*.

## How to actually use it

Look at patterns rather than single alerts. Has the same wallet been depositing roughly the same amount every week? Then one deposit is nothing special. Does a wallet that was quiet for years suddenly make a big deposit? That's more unusual. On a wallet page you can see exchange inflows and outflows per day, so you can spot such a pattern.
