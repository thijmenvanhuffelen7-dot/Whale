# What is smart money?

"Smart money" is a loose term for players who are assumed to know more or trade better than average: funds, market makers, early investors and traders with a long, visible track record. On a blockchain their transactions are public. That makes it possible to watch along.

## How do you recognize such a wallet?

A wallet has no name. Labels appear because someone links an address to a party: a fund announced it, the address received tokens from a known sale, or it shows a distinctive pattern. We use three kinds:

- **Smart money**: wallets with a long history of active, large trades.
- **Whale**: wallets holding a lot of capital, regardless of how they trade.
- **Exchange**: wallets of exchanges like Binance or Coinbase. We don't track those ourselves, but we use them to detect deposits and withdrawals.

## What does it mean when smart money buys?

Less than it seems. A purchase tells you *that* someone bought something, not *why*. It could be a long-term position, a hedge against something else, liquidity for a client, or a mistake. Large players often spread their trading over many wallets, so a single transaction is rarely the whole story.

## Pitfalls

- **You are always later.** By the time a transaction is in a block and reported, the price has often already moved.
- **Labels can be wrong.** A label is an estimate, not proof.
- **Survivorship bias.** You remember the times smart money was right.

So use smart-money data as context, not as a signal. It helps you ask better questions: who is moving, how much, and is that unusual for this wallet?
